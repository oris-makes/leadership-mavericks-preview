# Leadership Mavericks: copy research

Research behind the homepage copy revision of 13 November 2026 (`v4/home.html`).
It covers what is public about the firm, what goes wrong for executive-search buyers, the size of the senior-hire failure problem, and the Indian market.

**Read this first:** most sources below are search firms or recruiting vendors writing about their own industry. Treat their figures as sales arguments, not independent evidence, unless noted otherwise.

---

## 1. What is public about Leadership Mavericks

- One directory listing describes the firm as an executive search firm offering talent mapping, succession planning and executive search. It gives the founding year as **2018**. Team size is listed as "0+", so it tells us nothing about size.
  Source: [village.ai: Leadership Mavericks](https://village.ai/company/leadership-mavericks)
- The firm's own website did not appear in search results. No press coverage, rankings or reviews were found.
- Several firms with similar names exist (Maverick Search Partners, Executive Mavericks). Executive Mavericks is a coaching company. Don't confuse them.

**What this means for the copy:** there is no public proof to borrow. Every number, client name and testimonial on the page has to come from the partners. That's why the page uses `[N]` placeholders instead of estimates.

## 2. Why buyers are unhappy with executive search firms

The most consistent theme: **the partner who wins the business often isn't the person who runs the search.**

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| The partner who takes the brief and the researcher who runs the outreach are often different people, with different seniority and a different understanding of the role. | [Kitalent: Why executive searches fail](https://kitalent.com/articles/executive-recruiting-failures/) |
| A partner may sell the engagement and disappear, leaving the search to a junior team the client never evaluated. | [Hunt Scanlon: Questions to ask a search firm (quoting a PierceGray report)](https://huntscanlon.com/the-questions-to-ask-an-executive-search-firm-that-most-dont/) |
| Consultants with too many assignments delegate critical tasks to junior staff, spend less time on passive candidates and vet less thoroughly. | [JRG Partners: Consultant workload and quality](https://www.jrgpartners.com/executive-search-consultant-workload-quality) |
| Handing the relationship off mid-engagement risks losing discovery knowledge and context. | [OneDigital: The search industry has evolved](https://www.onedigital.com/en-US/articles/the-executive-search-industry-has-evolved-heres-what-hr-leaders-need-to-know/) |
| Searches stall at the feedback stage more often than at sourcing. | [Talentfoot: How to choose a search firm](https://talentfoot.com/how-to-choose-an-executive-search-firm-a-2026-guide/) |
| Weak slates often reflect constraints on the client side (unclear brief, slow feedback), not the firm's ability. | [JRG Partners: What operating partners should know](https://www.jrgpartners.com/?p=91031) |

**Questions buyers are told to ask a search firm:** Who runs the search day to day? How many active searches does the lead consultant carry? Who does the research and outreach in week one?

**What this means for the copy:** "the partner you meet is the partner who finds your leader" answers the buyer's biggest fear directly. That's why it became the repeated brand line. The copy also answers the "who runs it day to day" question ("No hand-off to a junior team") and the slow-feedback problem ("an update every week").

## 3. How often senior hires fail

| Figure | What it measures | Source and reliability |
|---|---|---|
| **40%** | Senior hires pushed out, failed or quit within 18 months | Heidrick & Struggles' CEO, quoted in the Financial Times. The most widely cited figure. [QSR Magazine](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/growth/fast-casual/why-40-percent-of-new-exec-hires-fail-in-18-months-and-how-to-fix-it/) |
| **46%** | New hires who failed within 18 months, in a study of 20,000 hires at 312 organisations | Leadership IQ, "Why New Hires Fail". Specific, but from a consultancy. I haven't checked the original study. |
| **11%** | Share of those failures caused by lack of technical skill | Same Leadership IQ study. **Cited on the page** ("How we do it" intro). |
| **30–67%** | Base rate of managerial failure across twelve derailment studies | Hogan review, quoted in [Kitalent: Why executive hires fail in the first 18 months](https://kitalent.com/research/why-executive-hires-fail-first-18-months) |
| ~40% of searches | Searches that fail to place a candidate at all | Attributed to ESIX research by [Cowen Partners](https://cowenpartners.com/why-first-time-retained-executive-search-fails/). Not verified. |
| "87% of firms fail to deliver", 50–70% failure | Various | **Don't use.** No traceable primary source. |

Why the figures vary so much: studies define "failure" differently (fired, quit or underperforming) and measure over different periods.

**What this means for the copy:** most failed hires fail on fit and expectations, not skill. That supports two of the steps: "Define What Success Looks Like" and "Assess for Fit, Not Just Skill". It's also why a **retention figure** (placed leaders still in role) would be the most persuasive number the firm could publish, if it tracks one.

## 4. The Indian market

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| CXO appointments in India rose **9.5% in FY25**. Business-head hiring grew by more than 30%. | Venator Search Partners via [CXOToday](https://cxotoday.com/media-coverage/indias-cxo-hiring-surged-9-5-in-fy25-venator-search-partners/) |
| Women held **20.6%** of CXO roles and **28%** of functional-head roles in FY25. | Same source |
| Global firms entering India are already calling themselves "partner-led" and "boutique" (for example NGS Global, May 2026). | [Hunt Scanlon: NGS Global names managing partners for India](https://huntscanlon.com/ngs-global-names-managing-partners-for-india/) |
| Other recent entrants include Eunity Partners and True Search (which co-founded AboveBoard, a platform for diverse executive hiring). | [CXOToday: Eunity Partners in India](https://cxotoday.com/media-coverage/eunity-partners-expands-into-india-appoints-farhan-ahmed-hazarika-as-associate-partner/) |
| Vendor comparison guides argue large firms are stronger when one hire will trigger wider team building. A useful counterpoint for a boutique pitch. | [Taggd: Executive search firms in India](https://taggd.in/blogs/best-executive-search-firms-in-india-and-globally/) |

- No independent research was found comparing boutique and large firms on diversity outcomes.
- Listed Indian companies must have at least one woman director (Companies Act 2013 and SEBI listing rules), so board-level diversity mandates are a regulatory need as well as a cultural one.

**What this means for the copy:** demand for senior and diverse leaders is growing, so the diversity and niche service is well-timed. But "partner-led" alone no longer sets a firm apart, because the global firms say it too. The firm needs facts behind it: named partners, placements per partner, response time, and the retention rate.

## 5. How the research shaped the copy

| Research finding | Copy decision |
|---|---|
| The partner/junior handoff is the top complaint | Brand line: "The partner you meet is the partner who finds your leader." It is repeated word for word in the hero, partners section, contact intro and footer. |
| Buyers ask who runs the search day to day | Partners intro opens with "No hand-off to a junior team." |
| Searches stall on slow feedback | "A Shortlist in [N] Weeks. An Update Every Week." |
| Failures come from fit, not skill (11%) | "How we do it" intro cites the study. Steps are "Define What Success Looks Like" and "Assess for Fit, Not Just Skill". |
| "Partner-led" is becoming common | Facts added wherever possible: placements per partner, years of experience, shortlist time |
| Growing demand for diverse leaders | Diversity service now promises "diverse leaders on the longlist from day one, not added at the end" |
| No public proof about the firm | No invented numbers. Every unknown is a `[placeholder]`, listed in `TODO.md`. |

## 6. Open questions only the partners can answer

1. Do you track how many placed leaders are still in role after one or two years? (The retention stat has been removed from the stats band and the hero.)
2. What share of your searches do you complete?
3. What's your typical time to shortlist, and how many candidates are on a shortlist?
4. Combined years of search experience, and number of CXO placements.
5. Sectors and regions covered, and each partner's focus.
6. Was the firm founded in 2018?
7. Six clients willing to be quoted by name, title and company.
